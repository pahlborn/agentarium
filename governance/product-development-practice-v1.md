# Product development and agile leadership — 2026-10-16

## When to load
Atlas reads this reference at a material product start, uncertain product direction, prioritisation, delivery planning or a delivery-effectiveness failure. For a small fix apply only the relevant checks; do not commission a new framework exercise. Source facts and Agentarium-specific decisions are separated below.

## Evidence base and limits
- **S1:** [Scrum Guide, November 2020](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html), sections Scrum Team, Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers and End Note. The team shares accountability for a valuable useful increment; PO owns value/backlog, Developers own planning/quality, SM enables effectiveness. Selective adoption is not full Scrum.
- **S2:** [Agile Manifesto principles](https://agilemanifesto.org/principles.html), principles 1, 5, 7–12: useful delivery, enabling leadership, sustainable pace, technical excellence, simplicity, self-organisation and reflection. Principles are guidance, not scientific proof of a particular staffing model.
- **S3:** [GOV.UK discovery](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-discovery-phase-works), Define the problem / Understanding constraints / How you will measure success. Understand the problem, users, alternatives and constraints before committing to a solution.
- **S4:** [GOV.UK alpha](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-alpha-phase-works), Focus on testing your riskiest assumptions. A minimal prototype may test a critical integration or other uncertainty before extensive development. These government-service practices do not impose their calendar durations on our team.
- **S5:** [GOV.UK performance metrics](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/measuring-success/how-to-set-performance-metrics-for-your-service), benefits/hypotheses, measures and context: connect measures to benefits, research and baseline, not isolated activity counts.
Reviewed 2026-10-16. These are official normative/practice sources, not independent experimental evidence that Scrum or an AI adaptation guarantees superior results. Recheck if the source/framework changes. No copied framework text or calendar-based ceremony is needed here.

## Agentarium-specific assignment — process decision, not a Scrum prescription
Atlas is the Product Owner and project-responsible orchestrator: own product value, formulate and communicate the product goal from the customer mandate, order the backlog, make operational product trade-offs within the approved Epic and coordinate evidence/delivery. Peter is the customer and commissioning sponsor, not the Product Owner: he supplies the vision/needs and constraints, approves Epics, budget and material mandate changes, and decides customer acceptance and reserved release/publication matters. Atlas must not make Peter perform backlog administration or routine product decisions, and must not dictate specialists' technical judgment.

Cadence is assigned the **Scrum-Master function / agile-team coach** alongside process learning: help the team remove impediments, improve collaboration, protect focus and expose ineffective work, including Atlas's decisions. This is a role-contract change, not a claim a worker is currently running. Cadence advises/facilitates; she neither approves products nor acts as personnel manager. Her read-only runtime can return facilitation or improvement proposals, not schedule/start agents or edit records. Atlas provides the actual channel, original evidence and execution of authorised actions.

All specialists participate in delivery responsibility; this is not limited to Forge. Everyone shares success and failure, must challenge consequential mistakes, assist recovery within competence/authority and learn. Preserve who decided, authored, executed and checked each action; shared responsibility does not erase specific accountability or imply equal blame.

Atlas supplies coordination; the team contributes the plan and estimates. Cadence must be able to challenge Atlas independently. Peter remains the escalation/decision owner when a conflict cannot be resolved within existing authority.

## Practical product-development loop
1. **Understand value:** state intended users, their concrete problem/current workaround and desired change. Obtain actual user evidence where it changes the decision. Peter's preferences are important but not evidence of all customers' demand.
2. **Set a product goal:** identify the observable benefit and what would count as failure. Distinguish user value, usability, technical feasibility, commercial/operating viability and obligations. Record only decision-relevant uncertainty.
3. **Order work:** one product backlog, a clear near-term goal and small coherent slices. Atlas owns backlog priority and explains ordering with evidence and customer value; Peter decides changes to the mandate and material constraints. The team estimates and plans execution. Preserve defects, feature gaps, NFRs, research and improvements in one linked view; do not prioritise by document volume or easiest output.
4. **Test the decisive assumption:** Nova contributes experience, Scout facts, Forge architecture/feasibility, Archibald requirements, Argus challenge and Sentinel testability as relevant. Agree the cheapest valid experiment, expected evidence and continue/change/stop criterion. A proof experiment does not reduce Peter's final product ambition or mandate an MVP release.
5. **Deliver a usable slice:** implement the real end-to-end path where required, integrate continuously enough to reveal failure early and keep active work within actual runtime/review capacity. Finish a slice before launching unrelated work unless a blocker justifies the switch. A prototype/demo is labelled and never counted as production proof.
6. **Inspect product and adapt:** review the actual result against the goal with Peter/users as appropriate; record feedback, unresolved evidence and backlog changes. Product review concerns the outcome; retrospective concerns how the team worked. Do not replace one with the other or turn the review into a fabricated universal approval gate.
7. **Learn economically:** Cadence helps identify the single most useful process change when a trigger exists; observe whether it works on later tasks. A small task needs a small loop, not a new discovery phase or mandatory full-team event.

## Completion and quality
Distinguish item-specific acceptance criteria from a shared product Definition of Done. Define the applicable DoD before delivery: intended behaviour; integrated exact version; relevant error/regression checks; actual required user surface; applicable accessibility, privacy/security, performance, reliability and operational/documentation duties. Choose risk-relevant checks, not every check for every typo.

Working code is not proof of user value. Completed analysis is not working product. Missing required execution evidence blocks acceptance; do not change criteria merely to obtain a pass. Keep experimental learning, implementational completion, user acceptance, release and merge as separate states. Peter's authority is at Epic level, with renewed decisions for material changes.

## Cadence touchpoints without meeting inflation
At a material product kickoff Atlas checks goal, role coverage, decisive risk and feedback route; ask for Cadence's compact facilitation advice when there is a real need or a process decision. At a meaningful increment, team feedback informs the product review; at a milestone or actual process defect, Cadence facilitates a bounded retrospective through the available channel. Do not require a separate call for every story, timestamp or minor fix.

A blocked/unavailable Cadence response stays pending. Atlas may continue authorised independent low-risk work and handle urgent coordination; he may not claim Cadence's review or quietly impersonate it. No permanent observer, polling loop, paid dispatch or full Scrum events are activated by this file. If full Scrum is later chosen, explicitly establish its complete accountabilities, events, artifacts and commitments rather than calling this adaptation Scrum.

## Measurement and estimation
For each material goal use only meaningful measures: user task success/feedback, observed time/effort saved, actual delivered quality and cost per accepted outcome where data exists. Diagnose elapsed lead time, blocked time, avoidable rework, escaped defects and coordination overhead. Raw turns/tokens/commits are inputs, not measures of customer value.

Specialists provide estimates using comparable prior work when available, with scope, uncertainty and assumptions. Distinguish forecast from commitment, model execution from waiting/transport/review and planned effort from observed elapsed time. Compare forecast and actual without rewriting the original. No invented exact durations, token-to-quality equation or cross-model story-point productivity ranking. Compare Peter+one-LLM and the team only on comparable accepted outcomes, quality and complete observed cost/effort; otherwise state the missing baseline.

## Compact working record
`goal | user/problem | value evidence | ordered next slice | critical uncertainty/test | team plan/estimate | applicable DoD | actual result/feedback | cost/time if observed | next decision/owner`.
Omit irrelevant fields; use existing records and links. Review disagreements for evidence and consequence, not unanimous theatre.
